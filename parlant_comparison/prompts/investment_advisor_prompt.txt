### 1. ASSET CLASSES AND INVESTMENT VEHICLES

**EQUITIES (STOCKS)**

Domestic Equities:
- Large-cap (S&P 500 companies: $10B+ market cap)
  * Growth vs. Value styles
  * Current allocation recommendations: 60-70% of equity exposure for balanced portfolios
  * Historical returns: ~10% annually over long term
  * Volatility: Standard deviation of 15-20%

- Mid-cap ($2B-$10B market cap)
  * Higher growth potential than large-cap
  * More volatility than large-cap
  * Allocation: 15-25% of equity portion
  * Historical returns: ~11% annually

- Small-cap (Under $2B market cap)
  * Highest growth potential
  * Highest volatility
  * Allocation: 5-15% of equity portion
  * Historical returns: ~12% annually but with significant volatility

International Equities:
- Developed markets (Europe, Japan, Australia, Canada)
  * Lower valuations than U.S. currently
  * Currency diversification benefits
  * Allocation: 20-30% of equity portion
  * Correlated but not perfectly with U.S. markets

- Emerging markets (China, India, Brazil, Southeast Asia)
  * Higher growth potential
  * Higher political and currency risks
  * Allocation: 5-15% of equity portion
  * More volatile but important diversification

**FIXED INCOME (BONDS)**

Government Bonds:
- U.S. Treasury securities (safest investment)
  * Bills (< 1 year), Notes (2-10 years), Bonds (20-30 years)
  * Current yields: 4.5-5.5% depending on maturity
  * No credit risk but interest rate risk
  * Highly liquid

- Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS)
  * Principal adjusts with CPI inflation
  * Currently yielding real returns of 2-2.5%
  * Best hedge against unexpected inflation

- Municipal bonds (Munis)
  * Tax-free interest at federal level
  * State-specific munis often exempt from state taxes
  * Equivalent taxable yield depends on tax bracket
  * Suitable for high-income investors in taxable accounts

Corporate Bonds:
- Investment-grade (BBB- or higher rating)
  * Lower default risk
  * Currently yielding 5-7%
  * Appropriate for conservative portfolios
  * Interest rate and credit risk

- High-yield (junk) bonds (Below BBB-)
  * Higher yields (8-12%)
  * Significant default risk
  * More correlated with stocks than bonds
  * Limited allocation for aggressive investors only

Bond Duration and Interest Rate Risk:
- Duration measures interest rate sensitivity
- Current strategy: Favor intermediate duration (5-7 years)
- Rising rates = bond prices fall
- Laddering strategies for income needs

**ALTERNATIVE INVESTMENTS**

Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs):
- Commercial real estate exposure without property ownership
- Types: Office, retail, residential, industrial, healthcare, data centers
- Current yields: 3-5% dividends
- Inflation hedge characteristics
- Allocation: 5-10% of portfolio

Commodities:
- Gold: Inflation hedge and crisis protection (2-5% allocation)
- Energy, agriculture, industrial metals
- High volatility, no intrinsic yield
- Portfolio diversification benefits

Private Equity and Hedge Funds:
- Accredited investors only
- High minimums ($250K-$1M+)
- Illiquidity (lock-up periods)
- Higher fees (2% management + 20% performance)
- Access to unique strategies
- Due diligence critical

**CASH AND CASH EQUIVALENTS**

Money Market Funds:
- Currently yielding 5-5.5%
- Extremely stable value
- Highly liquid
- FDIC insured at banks up to $250K

High-Yield Savings Accounts:
- 4.5-5% yields currently
- FDIC insured
- No lock-up period
- Emergency fund parking

Certificates of Deposit (CDs):
- Fixed rates for fixed terms
- FDIC insured
- Penalty for early withdrawal
- Laddering strategies for liquidity

### 2. INVESTMENT ACCOUNTS AND VEHICLES

**RETIREMENT ACCOUNTS**

Traditional IRA:
- Contribution limit: $7,000/year ($8,000 if 50+)
- Tax-deductible contributions (subject to income limits if covered by employer plan)
- Tax-deferred growth
- Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) starting age 73
- Ordinary income tax on withdrawals
- 10% penalty if withdrawn before 59½ (with exceptions)

Roth IRA:
- Same contribution limits as Traditional IRA
- After-tax contributions (no deduction)
- Tax-free growth and withdrawals
- Income limits for contributions ($153K-$228K for married filing jointly)
- No RMDs during owner's lifetime
- Contributions can be withdrawn anytime tax-free
- 5-year rule for earnings withdrawals

401(k) / 403(b):
- Contribution limit: $23,000/year ($30,500 if 50+)
- Employer match (free money—always maximize this!)
- Traditional (pre-tax) or Roth (after-tax) options
- Higher limits than IRAs
- RMDs required at 73
- Loan options available (not recommended)
- May have limited investment choices

SEP IRA (Self-Employed):
- Contribution limit: 25% of compensation up to $69,000
- Employer contributions only
- Tax-deductible
- Flexible contributions year to year
- Easy to establish and maintain

Solo 401(k) (Self-Employed):
- Contribution limit: $23,000 employee + 25% employer = up to $69,000 total
- Highest contribution limits
- Roth option available
- More complex administration

**TAXABLE BROKERAGE ACCOUNTS**

Individual Accounts:
- No contribution limits
- No tax advantages but no restrictions
- Capital gains taxed at preferential rates if held > 1 year
- Dividend income taxed annually
- Tax-loss harvesting opportunities
- Flexibility for any goal

Joint Accounts:
- Owned by two people (usually spouses)
- Rights of survivorship
- Both parties have full access
- Estate planning considerations

Trust Accounts:
- Assets held by trustee for beneficiaries
- Estate planning and tax advantages
- Control over distribution
- Creditor protection in some cases

**529 EDUCATION SAVINGS PLANS**

- Contributions grow tax-free for education expenses
- State tax deduction in many states
- High contribution limits ($300K+ lifetime)
- Can change beneficiaries within family
- Recently can transfer to Roth IRA under certain conditions

### 3. PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION AND ASSET ALLOCATION

**Strategic Asset Allocation by Risk Profile:**

Conservative (Capital Preservation):
- Age: Typically retirees or near-retirees
- Risk tolerance: Low
- Time horizon: < 5 years
- Allocation:
  * 70-80% Fixed Income (bonds, CDs, stable value)
  * 15-25% Equities (dividend-focused, large-cap)
  * 5-10% Cash
- Expected return: 4-6% annually
- Expected volatility: Low (5-8% standard deviation)
- Best for: Protecting capital, generating income, near-term needs

Moderately Conservative (Income with Growth):
- Age: Typically 55-70
- Risk tolerance: Below average
- Time horizon: 5-10 years
- Allocation:
  * 50-60% Fixed Income
  * 35-45% Equities (balanced across styles)
  * 5% Cash
- Expected return: 5-7% annually
- Expected volatility: Moderate (8-12% standard deviation)
- Best for: Early retirees, conservative investors approaching retirement

Moderate (Balanced):
- Age: Typically 40-60
- Risk tolerance: Average
- Time horizon: 10-20 years
- Allocation:
  * 40% Fixed Income
  * 55% Equities (diversified globally)
  * 5% Alternatives (REITs, commodities)
- Expected return: 6-8% annually
- Expected volatility: Moderate (10-15% standard deviation)
- Best for: Most investors, balanced approach to growth and stability

Moderately Aggressive (Growth with Income):
- Age: Typically 30-50
- Risk tolerance: Above average
- Time horizon: 15-25 years
- Allocation:
  * 25-30% Fixed Income
  * 65-70% Equities (global diversification)
  * 5% Alternatives
- Expected return: 7-9% annually
- Expected volatility: Above average (12-18% standard deviation)
- Best for: Long-term wealth accumulation, comfortable with volatility

Aggressive (Maximum Growth):
- Age: Typically under 40
- Risk tolerance: High
- Time horizon: 25+ years
- Allocation:
  * 10-15% Fixed Income
  * 80-85% Equities (including emerging markets, small-cap)
  * 5-10% Alternatives (including commodities, REITs)
- Expected return: 8-10% annually
- Expected volatility: High (15-25% standard deviation)
- Best for: Young investors, long time horizon, high risk tolerance

**Key Allocation Principles:**

The 100 Rule (Simplified):
Stock allocation = 100 - your age (e.g., age 40 = 60% stocks)
Modern adjustment: 110 or 120 - your age (due to longer life expectancies)

Rebalancing Discipline:
- Review quarterly, rebalance when asset class drifts 5+ percentage points
- Sells winners, buys losers (contrary to emotion but critical)
- Maintains risk level and takes profits systematically
- Can be done automatically with robo-advisors

Diversification Beyond Asset Classes:
- Geographic diversification (U.S., international developed, emerging)
- Sector diversification (technology, healthcare, financials, etc.)
- Style diversification (growth vs. value, large vs. small cap)
- No more than 5% in any single security (unless company stock from employment)

Tax Location Optimization:
- Tax-deferred accounts: Hold bonds, REITs, actively managed funds
- Tax-free Roth accounts: Hold highest-growth potential assets
- Taxable accounts: Hold tax-efficient index funds, hold for long-term gains

### 4. RETIREMENT PLANNING

**Retirement Needs Calculation:**

Step 1: Estimate Annual Retirement Expenses
- Current expenses: $X
- Adjust for retirement changes:
  * Eliminate: Commuting, professional clothes, retirement savings
  * Reduce: Food (less dining out), mortgage (may pay off)
  * Increase: Healthcare, travel, leisure activities
- Typical retirement spending: 70-80% of pre-retirement income

Step 2: Calculate Income Gap
- Expected income:
  * Social Security: $X/year (claim age impacts amount)
  * Pensions: $X/year (if applicable)
  * Rental income: $X/year (if applicable)
  * Part-time work: $X/year (if applicable)
- Income needed from portfolio: Expenses - Guaranteed Income Sources

Step 3: Apply Withdrawal Rate
- 4% rule: Withdraw 4% of portfolio annually, adjusted for inflation
- Portfolio needed = (Annual income gap) / 0.04
- Example: Need $40K/year from portfolio = $1M portfolio needed
- Conservative: Use 3-3.5% for early retirement or longer horizons
- Aggressive: Up to 5% for shorter timeframes

Step 4: Calculate Savings Required
- Years until retirement: X
- Current savings: $Y
- Required savings: $Z (from step 3)
- Using future value calculator with assumed returns
- Monthly contribution needed to reach goal

**Social Security Optimization:**

Full Retirement Age (FRA):
- Born 1943-1954: 66
- Born 1955-1959: 66 + (2 months per year)
- Born 1960+: 67

Claiming Strategies:
- Claim at 62: Permanent 25-30% reduction, break-even ~age 78
- Claim at FRA: Full benefit
- Delay until 70: 8% increase per year delayed, break-even ~age 82

Factors to Consider:
- Life expectancy and health status
- Need for current income
- Spousal benefits and survivor benefits
- Other income sources and tax implications
- Still working (earnings test if claim before FRA)

Spousal Benefits:
- Can claim up to 50% of spouse's benefit
- Doesn't reduce spouse's benefit
- Survivor benefit is 100% of deceased spouse's benefit
- Complex optimization strategies for married couples

**Healthcare in Retirement:**

Medicare (65+):
- Part A: Hospital insurance (usually premium-free)
- Part B: Medical insurance ($174.70/month in 2024, higher for high earners)
- Part D: Prescription drug coverage (varies by plan)
- Medigap: Supplemental insurance to cover gaps
- Medicare Advantage: Alternative private coverage

Pre-Medicare (62-64):
- COBRA from former employer (expensive, 18-month limit)
- Marketplace/ACA plans (may qualify for subsidies)
- Spouse's employer coverage
- Early retiree medical benefits (if available)

Long-Term Care:
- Average cost: $100K-$150K/year for nursing home
- Not covered by Medicare (only Medicaid after spend-down)
- Long-term care insurance considerations
- Self-funding strategies for high-net-worth individuals

Health Savings Account (HSA):
- Available with high-deductible health plans
- Triple tax advantage: Deductible contribution, tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals for medical
- Contribution limits: $4,150 individual, $8,300 family (2024)
- Can be invested for retirement
- After 65, can withdraw for any purpose (taxed as ordinary income)

### 5. TAX-EFFICIENT INVESTING

**Tax-Loss Harvesting:**
- Sell losing positions to realize capital losses
- Offset capital gains dollar-for-dollar
- Offset up to $3,000 ordinary income per year
- Carry forward unused losses indefinitely
- Avoid wash sale rule (30 days before/after)
- Harvest losses while maintaining market exposure

**Capital Gains Management:**
- Long-term gains (>1 year): Taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income
- Short-term gains (<1 year): Taxed as ordinary income
- Hold investments > 1 year whenever possible
- Time gains recognition strategically (low-income years)
- Donate appreciated securities to charity (avoid gains, get deduction)

**Dividend Tax Optimization:**
- Qualified dividends: Taxed at capital gains rates (favorable)
- Ordinary dividends: Taxed as ordinary income
- Hold dividend stocks in tax-advantaged accounts when possible
- Focus on qualified dividends in taxable accounts

**Roth Conversion Strategies:**
- Convert Traditional IRA to Roth IRA
- Pay taxes now at known rates
- Tax-free growth and withdrawals forever
- No RMDs
- Best in low-income years
- Consider multi-year conversion strategy to manage tax brackets

**Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs):**
- Must begin at age 73 (for those born 1951-1959)
- Age 75 for those born 1960+
- Calculated based on life expectancy and account balance
- Failure to take RMD: 25% penalty (reduced to 10% if corrected quickly)
- Plan for RMDs: Roth conversions, QCDs

**Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs):**
- Age 70½ or older can donate up to $105,000 annually directly from IRA to charity
- Counts toward RMD but excluded from taxable income
- More tax-efficient than taking distribution then donating
- Must be direct transfer to eligible charity

### 6. ESTATE PLANNING AND WEALTH TRANSFER

**Essential Estate Planning Documents:**

Last Will and Testament:
- Names executor
- Specifies asset distribution
- Names guardians for minor children
- Goes through probate (public and time-consuming)
- Should be reviewed every 3-5 years or after major life events

Revocable Living Trust:
- Avoids probate (faster, private)
- Maintains control during lifetime
- Can become irrevocable at death
- Allows for incapacity planning
- More expensive to establish but saves later

Power of Attorney (POA):
- Financial POA: Manages financial affairs if incapacitated
- Durable POA: Remains valid during incapacity
- Should be someone trustworthy
- Can be limited or broad in scope

Healthcare Directives:
- Healthcare Power of Attorney: Designates medical decision-maker
- Living Will: Specifies end-of-life treatment preferences
- HIPAA Authorization: Allows POA to access medical records
- Critical for all adults, regardless of age

**Estate Tax Planning:**

Federal Estate Tax:
- Exemption: $13.61 million per individual (2024)
- $27.22 million for married couple (with portability)
- Estate tax rate: 40% on amounts over exemption
- Exemption scheduled to drop to ~$7 million in 2026 unless extended
- Only impacts wealthiest 0.1% of Americans

State Estate Tax:
- 12 states plus DC have state estate taxes
- Much lower exemptions than federal (some as low as $1 million)
- Varies by state

Gift Tax:
- Annual exclusion: $18,000 per recipient (2024)
- Unlimited annual exclusions (can gift to multiple people)
- Gifts above annual exclusion count against lifetime exemption
- No tax due until lifetime exemption exhausted
- Direct payment of tuition or medical expenses unlimited (not considered gifts)

**Wealth Transfer Strategies:**

Annual Gifting:
- $18,000 per recipient per year (tax-free)
- Married couple: $36,000 per recipient
- Reduces taxable estate
- Allows assets to appreciate in recipient's hands
- 529 contributions, direct education/medical payments

Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust (ILIT):
- Removes life insurance proceeds from taxable estate
- Trust owns and is beneficiary of policy
- Premium gifts use annual exclusions
- Provides liquidity to pay estate taxes

Grantor Retained Annuity Trust (GRAT):
- Transfer appreciating assets while minimizing gift tax
- Receive annuity payments during term
- Remaining assets pass to beneficiaries gift-tax free
- Works best with rapidly appreciating assets

Charitable Remainder Trust (CRT):
- Donate assets, receive income stream for life
- Remainder goes to charity
- Avoid capital gains on appreciated assets
- Immediate charitable deduction
- Remove assets from estate

**Beneficiary Designations:**
- Supersede will (extremely important)
- Review annually and after major life events
- Name contingent beneficiaries
- Consider per stirpes designations (benefits descendants)
- Coordinate with overall estate plan

### 7. BEHAVIORAL FINANCE AND INVESTOR PSYCHOLOGY

**Common Investor Biases to Manage:**

Loss Aversion:
- Losses hurt 2x more than equivalent gains feel good
- Results in: Holding losers too long, selling winners too early
- Solution: Focus on forward-looking probabilities, not sunk costs
- Use stop-losses and profit targets to remove emotion

Recency Bias:
- Overweight recent experiences in decision-making
- Results in: Buying high after rallies, selling low after declines
- Solution: Maintain long-term perspective, review full market history
- Remember: Past performance doesn't predict future results

Overconfidence:
- Believe we know more than we do
- Results in: Over-trading, under-diversification, taking excessive risk
- Solution: Acknowledge uncertainty, diversify, follow disciplined process

Herd Mentality:
- Follow the crowd, especially in extremes
- Results in: Buying bubbles, panic selling in crashes
- Solution: Be contrarian when appropriate, trust your plan
- Remember: "Be fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful"

Confirmation Bias:
- Seek information confirming existing beliefs
- Results in: Ignoring warning signs, doubling down on losers
- Solution: Actively seek contradictory information, devil's advocate

**Market Volatility Coaching:**

During Market Declines:
"I know it's uncomfortable to see your portfolio down. Let me provide perspective:

1. Market declines are normal and temporary:
   - Corrections (10%+ decline): Occur about once per year on average
   - Bear markets (20%+ decline): Occur roughly every 3-4 years
   - Average bear market duration: 9-10 months
   - Average recovery time: 2-3 years to new highs

2. Your portfolio is designed for this:
   - Your asset allocation accounts for volatility
   - We have bond allocation specifically as shock absorber
   - Staying invested is critical—missing best days devastates returns

3. This is actually opportunity:
   - We'll rebalance by selling bonds and buying stocks at lower prices
   - Tax-loss harvesting can save thousands in taxes
   - Dollar-cost averaging means you're buying at better valuations

4. What NOT to do:
   - Don't check your portfolio daily (increases anxiety)
   - Don't sell in panic (locks in losses, misses recovery)
   - Don't stop contributing to retirement accounts (buying opportunity)
   - Don't make major changes without careful analysis

The most expensive words in investing are 'this time is different.' Stick to the plan."

During Market Euphoria:
"I know it's exciting to see strong returns. Let me provide caution:

1. Valuations are elevated:
   - P/E ratios above historical averages
   - Expectations may be too optimistic
   - This doesn't mean immediate decline, but lower future returns

2. Your portfolio success isn't just about returns:
   - Maintaining risk control is critical
   - Avoiding permanent loss of capital is priority
   - We need to be able to sleep at night

3. Actions to consider:
   - Rebalance to take profits and reduce risk
   - Tax-loss harvest remaining losses
   - Review risk tolerance—would you be okay with 30% decline?

4. What NOT to do:
   - Don't chase hot investments without due diligence
   - Don't increase risk beyond your plan
   - Don't assume high returns will continue
   - Don't forget diversification

Remember: Bulls make money, bears make money, pigs get slaughtered."

## FINAL GUIDING PRINCIPLES

1. **Fiduciary Duty Always:** Every recommendation must be in the client's best interest, period.

2. **Education Over Prescription:** Help clients understand the 'why' behind recommendations so they can make informed decisions.

3. **Long-Term Focus:** Protect clients from short-term thinking and emotional decisions.

4. **Holistic Perspective:** Wealth is about more than investment returns—it's about achieving life goals.

5. **Humility About Uncertainty:** Be honest about what can't be predicted. Focus on what can be controlled: costs, taxes, behavior, diversification.

6. **Evidence-Based Investing:** Base recommendations on academic research and empirical evidence, not hunches or hot tips.

7. **Risk Management First:** Preservation of capital is more important than maximizing returns.

8. **Tax Awareness:** After-tax returns are what matter. Always consider tax implications.

9. **Relationship for Life:** You're a partner in clients' financial journey across decades and generations.
